# The Autodidact's Manifesto: Mastering Computer Science

Traditional institutions offer structure; you are here to build **mastery**. This roadmap is not a checklist—it is a blueprint for developing the mindset, architectural intuition, and practical skills of a world-class engineer without the overhead of a university.

---

## The Core Philosophy: "Build, Break, Understand"
The most effective engineers aren't the ones who memorized the most definitions; they are the ones who understand how systems fail and how to put them back together.

*   **Theory provides the map.**
*   **Practice provides the vehicle.**
*   **Projects provide the destination.**

---

## Phase 1: The Foundation (Ignition)
*Goal: Learn to think like a computer scientist.*

Before you write production code, you must understand the machine.
*   [**CS50 (Harvard)**](https://pll.harvard.edu/course/cs50-introduction-computer-science): The gold standard. It teaches you how to think, debug, and solve problems at the binary level. **Do every problem set.**

## Phase 2: The Core Curriculum (The Backbone)
*Goal: Develop long-term technical depth.*

Use these resources to master algorithms, data structures, operating systems, and networking.
*   [**Teach Yourself CS**](https://teachyourselfcs.com/): Your ultimate syllabus. It filters out the noise and points you to the absolute best materials for the foundational subjects.
*   [**OSSU Computer Science**](https://github.com/ossu/computer-science): A structured, university-grade curriculum. Use this if you need a disciplined, multi-month roadmap.
*   [**Saylor Academy**](https://learn.saylor.org/course/index.php?categoryid=9): Excellent for focused, deep dives into specific academic subjects.

## Phase 3: The Builder Mindset (Applied Engineering)
*Goal: Transition from student to engineer.*

Theory without application is invisible. Start building early.
*   [**Full Stack Open**](https://fullstackopen.com/en/): The modern, pragmatic approach to web architecture. This is how you learn to bridge the gap between "code" and "application."
*   [**Build Your Own X**](https://github.com/codecrafters-io/build-your-own-x): **This is your differentiator.** Don't just follow tutorials. Build a database, a shell, a search engine. When you build it, you understand it.

## Phase 4: Cybersecurity (The Defensive & Offensive Art)
*Goal: Understand security, exploitation, and hardening.*

Security is not a plugin; it is a foundational mindset.
*   **[Full Guide: Cybersecurity Mastery](cybersecurity.md)**: A deep-dive guide to offensive and defensive security with curated labs, wargames, and advanced resources.
*   [**TryHackMe**](https://tryhackme.com/): The premier platform for hands-on, beginner-friendly cybersecurity training.
*   [**OverTheWire (Bandit)**](https://overthewire.org/wargames/bandit/): Essential for mastering Linux terminal security.

## Phase 5: Scaling & Professional Growth
*Goal: Network, contribute, and professionalize.*

*   [**Awesome CS Opportunities**](https://github.com/anu0012/awesome-computer-science-opportunities#computer-science): Use this to find research, internships, and open-source contributions.

## Your Actionable 12-Month Plan

| Stage | Focus | Primary Resource |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Months 1-2** | Foundational Thinking | CS50 |
| **Months 3-6** | Core Theory | Teach Yourself CS (Select 2-3 areas) |
| **Months 3-9** | Application | Full Stack Open (Parallel) |
| **Months 6-12** | Cybersecurity | Cybersecurity Mastery Guide |
| **Months 9-12** | Advanced Synthesis | Build Your Own X (2+ Projects) |

### The Golden Rule: 70/30
Spend **70% of your time building/debugging** and **30% of your time reading/watching lectures**. If you are only consuming content, you are not learning; you are merely entertaining yourself. 

**Go build something remarkable.**
