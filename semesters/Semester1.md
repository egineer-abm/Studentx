# Semester 1: Foundations of Engineering & Computing

## Information and Communication Technologies (ICT)

### Overview
Introductory course covering the fundamental concepts of information technology, communication systems, and digital literacy. Provides a foundation for understanding how computers and networks operate in modern organizations.

### Learning Objectives
- Understand the basic components of computer systems (hardware, software, firmware)
- Comprehend data representation and number systems (binary, octal, hexadecimal)
- Understand computer networks, the Internet, and basic networking concepts
- Gain proficiency in productivity software (MS Office/Google Workspace)
- Understand information systems and their role in organizations
- Develop skills in digital communication, collaboration, and online research
- Understand cybersecurity fundamentals and safe computing practices
- Learn about emerging technologies (cloud computing, IoT, AI, blockchain)

### Detailed Topics
- **Module 1:** Introduction to Computers: history, generations, types (micro, mini, mainframe, super)
- **Module 2:** Hardware Components: CPU, memory (RAM, ROM, cache), storage (HDD, SSD, optical), I/O devices
- **Module 3:** Software: system software (OS, drivers, utilities), application software, open source vs. proprietary
- **Module 4:** Number Systems: binary, octal, hexadecimal, conversions, binary arithmetic, 1's/2's complement
- **Module 5:** Data Representation: text (ASCII, Unicode), images (bitmap, vector), audio, video
- **Module 6:** Computer Networks: LAN, WAN, MAN, topologies (star, bus, ring, mesh), OSI model, TCP/IP
- **Module 7:** Internet and Web: browsers, search engines, email, cloud services, web 2.0, social media
- **Module 8:** Productivity Software: word processing, spreadsheets, presentations, databases (MS Office/Google Workspace)
- **Module 9:** Cybersecurity: threats (malware, phishing, social engineering), protection (antivirus, firewalls, encryption), safe browsing, password management
- **Module 10:** Emerging Technologies: cloud computing (IaaS, PaaS, SaaS), IoT, AI, blockchain, big data
- **Module 11:** Digital Ethics: copyright, plagiarism, netiquette, digital footprint, privacy

## Computer Engineering Workshop

### Overview
Hands-on workshop introducing engineering tools, practices, and methodologies. Covers workshop safety, basic fabrication techniques, electronic components, and engineering documentation.

### Learning Objectives
- Understand workshop safety practices and procedures
- Learn to use basic hand tools and measuring instruments
- Understand electronic components: resistors, capacitors, diodes, transistors, ICs
- Develop skills in soldering, PCB design, and circuit assembly
- Learn to use test equipment: multimeter, oscilloscope, function generator
- Create engineering drawings and documentation
- Develop problem-solving and troubleshooting skills

### Detailed Topics
- **Module 1:** Workshop Safety: safety rules, hazard identification, personal protective equipment (PPE), fire safety, first aid
- **Module 2:** Hand Tools: screwdrivers, pliers, cutters, wrenches, hammers, measuring tools (calipers, micrometers)
- **Module 3:** Power Tools: drills, grinders, saws, soldering stations — safe operation and maintenance
- **Module 4:** Electronic Components: identification, specifications, datasheets, resistors (color codes), capacitors, inductors, diodes, LEDs, transistors, ICs
- **Module 5:** Soldering: through-hole soldering, desoldering, surface mount (SMD) basics, soldering iron maintenance
- **Module 6:** PCB Design and Fabrication: schematic capture, PCB layout (Eagle, KiCad), etching, drilling, assembly
- **Module 7:** Test Equipment: digital multimeter (DMM), oscilloscope, function generator, DC power supply, logic probe
- **Module 8:** Measurement Techniques: voltage, current, resistance, continuity, frequency, waveform analysis
- **Module 9:** Engineering Drawings: orthographic projections, isometric views, dimensioning, tolerances, CAD software
- **Module 10:** Arduino UNO and IoT: Arduino board architecture (ATmega328P), digital/analog I/O pins, PWM, serial communication, breadboard prototyping, sensor interfacing (temperature, ultrasonic, light, motion), actuator control (LEDs, motors, servos), IoT basics (ESP8266/ESP32, WiFi, MQTT, cloud platforms), introductory Arduino programming (setup/loop, digitalWrite, analogRead, delay, Serial)
- **Module 11:** Project Management: planning, documentation, bill of materials, assembly instructions, testing procedures

## Calculus and Analytical Geometry

### Overview
First course in calculus covering differential and integral calculus of functions of a single variable. Emphasizes conceptual understanding, computational skills, and real-world applications in engineering.

### Learning Objectives
- Understand limits, continuity, and the formal definition of a derivative
- Compute derivatives using differentiation rules
- Apply differentiation to optimization, related rates, and curve sketching
- Understand the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
- Compute integrals using various techniques
- Apply integration to area, volume, and physical applications
- Understand sequences, series, and introductory Taylor expansions

### Detailed Topics
- **Module 1:** Limits and Continuity: limit laws, squeeze theorem, intermediate value theorem, epsilon-delta definition
- **Module 2:** Derivatives: definition, power rule, product/quotient rule, chain rule, implicit differentiation, logarithmic differentiation
- **Module 3:** Applications of Derivatives: optimization, related rates, curve sketching, L'Hôpital's rule, Mean Value Theorem, Newton's method
- **Module 4:** Integrals: Riemann sums, definite and indefinite integrals, Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
- **Module 5:** Integration Techniques: substitution, integration by parts, trigonometric integrals, trigonometric substitution, partial fractions
- **Module 6:** Applications of Integration: area between curves, volumes of revolution (disk, washer, shell), arc length, surface area, work, hydrostatic force
- **Module 7:** Sequences and Series: convergence tests, power series, Taylor and Maclaurin series, binomial series

## Applied Physics

### Overview
Introductory physics course covering classical mechanics and basic electricity/magnetism, with emphasis on engineering applications and problem-solving using calculus.

### Learning Objectives
- Understand Newtonian mechanics: kinematics, dynamics, energy, momentum, rotational motion
- Analyze physical systems using free-body diagrams and conservation laws
- Understand electric fields, potentials, circuits, and basic magnetism
- Apply calculus to solve physics problems
- Develop laboratory skills: measurement, data analysis, error analysis, report writing

### Detailed Topics (Mechanics)
- **Module 1:** Kinematics: 1D and 2D motion, vectors, projectile motion, relative motion
- **Module 2:** Newton's Laws: forces, friction, tension, circular motion, gravitation
- **Module 3:** Work and Energy: work-energy theorem, kinetic/potential energy, conservation of energy, power
- **Module 4:** Momentum and Collisions: impulse, conservation of momentum, elastic/inelastic collisions, center of mass
- **Module 5:** Rotational Motion: angular kinematics, torque, moment of inertia, angular momentum, rolling motion

### Detailed Topics (Electricity & Magnetism)
- **Module 6:** Electric Charge and Fields: Coulomb's law, electric field, Gauss's law, electric potential
- **Module 7:** Capacitance and Dielectrics: capacitors, energy storage, dielectrics
- **Module 8:** Current and Circuits: Ohm's law, resistivity, Kirchhoff's laws, RC circuits
- **Module 9:** Magnetic Fields: Lorentz force, Biot-Savart law, Ampere's law, Faraday's law, inductance

### Books
- **"University Physics with Modern Physics, 15th Edition"** by Young and Freedman (Pearson, 2019) — Standard engineering physics textbook
- **"Fundamentals of Physics, 12th Edition"** by Halliday, Resnick, and Walker (Wiley, 2021)
