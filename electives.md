## Semester 7 Elective: Applied Generative AI

### Applied Generative AI (AGAI)
- **Overview:** Comprehensive course covering the practical applications of Generative AI including Large Language Models (LLMs), text-to-image generation, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), AI agents, and responsible AI deployment. Prepares students to build, deploy, and evaluate generative AI systems in real-world scenarios.
- **Topics:**
  - **Module 1:** Foundations of Generative AI: principles, evolution from GANs to Transformers, autoregressive models, diffusion models, foundation models
  - **Module 2:** Large Language Models (LLMs): GPT architecture, transformer attention mechanisms, prompt engineering (zero-shot, few-shot, chain-of-thought), fine-tuning (LoRA, QLoRA, PEFT), instruction tuning, RLHF
  - **Module 3:** Text-to-Image Generation: Stable Diffusion, DALL·E, Midjourney, ControlNet, image inpainting, prompt engineering for images
  - **Module 4:** Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG): vector databases (Pinecone, Chroma, Weaviate), embedding models, document chunking, hybrid search, RAG evaluation, advanced RAG (Graph RAG, Agentic RAG)
  - **Module 5:** AI Agents and Tool Use: LangChain, LangGraph, AutoGPT, function calling, tool orchestration, multi-agent systems, planning and reasoning
  - **Module 6:** Multimodal AI: vision-language models (CLIP, LLaVA, GPT-4V), audio generation, video generation (Sora, Runway), multimodal RAG
  - **Module 7:** Generative AI for Code: GitHub Copilot, CodeLlama, automated code generation, code review, testing, documentation generation
  - **Module 8:** Fine-Tuning and Customization: supervised fine-tuning, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, dataset preparation, evaluation metrics, model deployment
  - **Module 9:** Responsible AI: bias detection and mitigation, safety filters, content moderation, jailbreaking prevention, watermarking, AI ethics, regulatory compliance (EU AI Act)
  - **Module 10:** Production Deployment: model serving (vLLM, TGI, Ollama), API design, cost optimization, caching, monitoring, A/B testing, MLOps for GenAI
- **Tools:** Python, PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers, LangChain, LangGraph, LlamaIndex, ChromaDB, Pinecone, Weaviate, vLLM, Ollama, ComfyUI, Docker, Kubernetes
- **Books:**

## Semester 7 Elective: Cognitive Computing

### Cognitive Computing (CC)
- **Overview:** Study of cognitive computing systems that emulate human thought processes, combining artificial intelligence, neuroscience, psychology, and decision science. Covers cognitive architectures, knowledge representation, reasoning under uncertainty, and human-AI interaction.
- **Topics:**
  - **Module 1:** Introduction to Cognitive Computing: definition, history, cognitive vs. traditional AI, cognitive systems characteristics (contextual, adaptive, iterative, stateful)
  - **Module 2:** Cognitive Architectures: SOAR, ACT-R, Sigma, Nengo, comparison of symbolic vs. connectionist vs. hybrid approaches
  - **Module 3:** Knowledge Representation and Reasoning: semantic networks, frames, ontologies, description logic, graph databases (Neo4j), knowledge graphs, Cyc, WordNet, ConceptNet
  - **Module 4:** Natural Language Understanding: syntax, semantics, pragmatics, discourse analysis, named entity recognition, relation extraction, coreference resolution, NLU pipelines
  - **Module 5:** Neural Cognitive Models: neural networks for cognitive tasks, memory-augmented neural networks (NTM, DNC), transformer models for reasoning, neuro-symbolic AI
  - **Module 6:** Perception and Pattern Recognition: visual cognition, object recognition, scene understanding, auditory perception, multi-sensory integration
  - **Module 7:** Learning and Adaptation: supervised, unsupervised, reinforcement learning, one-shot learning, transfer learning, continual learning, curriculum learning
  - **Module 8:** Reasoning Under Uncertainty: Bayesian networks, probabilistic graphical models, Markov logic networks, Dempster-Shafer theory, fuzzy logic, Monte Carlo methods
  - **Module 9:** Decision Making: decision theory, utility theory, game theory, multi-criteria decision analysis, cognitive biases, behavioral economics, explainable AI (XAI)
  - **Module 10:** Human-AI Interaction: cognitive load theory, mental models, trust in AI, human-in-the-loop systems, collaborative AI, affective computing, conversational AI
  - **Module 11:** Applications: intelligent tutoring systems, cognitive assistants, healthcare decision support, autonomous vehicles, robotic cognition, financial trading systems, smart cities
  - **Module 12:** Ethics and Future Directions: cognitive liberty, AI consciousness debate, ethical considerations, cognitive enhancement, whole brain emulation, responsible cognitive computing
- **Tools:** Python, TensorFlow, PyTorch, Neo4j (graph databases), Nengo (neural modeling), SOAR cognitive architecture, ACT-R, scikit-learn, NetworkX, spaCy, Prolog, R

## Custom Course Demand by Students

### Overview
Students can propose and design custom elective courses based on emerging technologies, industry demands, or special research interests. This section lists courses that have been requested or could be offered based on student demand.

### How to Propose a Custom Course
1. **Identify a Gap:** Find a technology or domain area not covered by existing courses
2. **Gather Support:** Form a group of at least 5-10 interested students
3. **Propose a Syllabus:** Define topics, learning objectives, assessment methods
4. **Find a Faculty Advisor:** Identify a faculty member willing to supervise
5. **Submit for Approval:** Submit proposal to the academic committee for review

### Custom Course Approval Process
1. **Proposal Submission:** Submit a detailed proposal (2-3 pages) including course title, description, learning objectives, weekly topics, assessment methods, and resources
2. **Faculty Review:** A faculty committee reviews the proposal for academic rigor and feasibility
3. **Resource Assessment:** Determine if required hardware, software, and faculty expertise are available
4. **Student Interest Survey:** Minimum 10 students must register interest for the course to run
5. **Approval and Scheduling:** Approved courses are scheduled for the next semester
6. **Pilot Offering:** First offering is treated as a pilot with post-course evaluation

### Student Innovation Fund
Students can apply for a small grant (up to $500) to develop course materials, purchase hardware/software, or organize workshops for custom courses they propose. This encourages student-driven curriculum innovation.
